Silk vs. Fresh Wedding Flowers: What They Really Cost

A real comparison of bridal bouquets, centerpieces, and full wedding packages — silk/faux rental versus fresh — and what actually drives the price.

Short Answer

Faux Rental Typically Runs 40-60% Less

A full fresh wedding florals package — bridal party bouquets, ceremony arrangements, and reception centerpieces for every table — commonly runs $2,000 to $4,000 or more from a professional florist once delivery and setup fees are added. A comparable faux or silk rental package from a local studio typically runs $800 to $2,200 for the same scope, which is where the 40-60% savings estimate comes from.

The gap holds up whether you're comparing individual pieces or a full package. A rented silk bridal bouquet typically runs $85-$225, versus $150-$400+ for a comparable fresh bouquet. Rented centerpieces run $45-$150 per table, versus fresh centerpieces that commonly run $75-$250+ per table depending on flower variety and season.

What Changes the Price

Five Factors That Move Your Number

Guest & Table Count

More reception tables means more centerpieces — the single biggest driver of total cost either way.

Personal Flower Count

A larger wedding party means more bouquets, boutonnières, and corsages to design and rent.

Ceremony Installations

A full arch or backdrop installation adds noticeably more than aisle décor alone.

Season (Fresh Only)

Fresh flower cost swings with what's in bloom and shipping availability; faux pricing stays consistent year-round.

Design Complexity

A simple, single-variety look costs less than a dense, multi-textured arrangement either way.

Realism

Do Faux Flowers Actually Look as Good?

Modern real-touch silk and faux florals are a different product entirely from the stiff, shiny artificial flowers of a decade ago. Hand-shaded petals, weighted stems, and real-flower proportions mean today's faux arrangements photograph the same as fresh — and because they don't wilt, they often look better in photos taken late in the reception than fresh flowers do by that point.

Mix & Match

You Don't Have to Choose Just One

A lot of couples split the difference: renting faux centerpieces and bridal party flowers for the bulk of their florals, while adding a small amount of fresh greenery or a few real stems near the ceremony for fragrance. It's a practical way to capture most of the savings while keeping a touch of fresh floral scent where guests notice it most.

Common Questions

Cost FAQ

Is renting silk wedding flowers actually cheaper than fresh?

Yes, in most cases. A full fresh wedding florals package commonly runs $2,000-$4,000+, while a comparable silk/faux rental package typically runs $800-$2,200 — roughly 40-60% less.

What is the average cost of a bridal bouquet — silk vs. fresh?

A rented silk bridal bouquet typically runs $85-$225. A comparable fresh bridal bouquet commonly runs $150-$400 or more depending on flower type and season.

Do silk or faux flowers really look as good as fresh in photos?

Modern real-touch silk and faux florals are designed to photograph the same as fresh flowers, and since they won't wilt, they often look better in late-reception photos.

What drives wedding flower cost up or down?

Guest and table count, personal flower count, ceremony installations, season (for fresh flowers), and design complexity — all covered above.

Can I mix fresh and faux flowers to save money?

Yes — many couples rent faux centerpieces and bridal party flowers while adding fresh greenery or a few real stems for fragrance.
