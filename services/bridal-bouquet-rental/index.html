Styles

Wedding Bouquet Styles You Can Rent

Most of our bouquet rentals fall into five shapes. Any of them can be built in your colors, and bridesmaid bouquets usually take a smaller, simpler version of the bride's shape.

  • Round or dome: tightly gathered roses, ranunculus and spray roses. It's the classic choice for ballroom receptions downtown and hotel weddings.
  • Garden or hand-tied: looser and asymmetrical, with trailing greenery and visible stems. It works well for barn and outdoor venues around Edmond, Guthrie and Norman.
  • Cascade: flowers that spill downward from the hand. This style is heavier and more formal. Faux stems keep it light enough to hold through a long ceremony.
  • Posy or nosegay: small and compact. It's a common pick for courthouse weddings, elopements and junior bridesmaids.
  • Dried-look or boho: pampas, bunny tails, eucalyptus and muted tones. Real dried bouquets shed and crumble, and faux versions of this style don't.
How It Works

How Renting a Wedding Bouquet Works

1. Consultation. Bring your color swatch, a photo of your dress and any inspiration pictures. We show you finished pieces in person and agree on stem choices and sizes for the bride and each bridesmaid.

2. Written quote and date hold. Your quote lists every bouquet, boutonnière and corsage, along with the pickup and return details. If you want to keep the bridal bouquet, the purchase option goes on the quote too.

3. Pickup before the wedding. Bouquets are ready a few days ahead, so they can go to your hair-and-makeup location or the venue with everything else. There's no need to refrigerate them and nothing to keep watered.

4. Return after the wedding. We arrange a pickup, or you or a bridesmaid drop the pieces off at a scheduled time. Normal handling, toss photos and a hot afternoon are all expected. Ask about the terms for lost or badly damaged pieces when you book, so nobody is surprised.

Why Faux in Oklahoma

Oklahoma City Weather Is Hard on Fresh Bouquets

Most Oklahoma City weddings fall in two windows, April through June and September through October. Both can turn hot, and spring brings strong, gusty wind. A fresh bouquet held through outdoor portraits at Myriad Botanical Gardens or Scissortail Park in a 90-degree June afternoon will droop before the reception. Hydrangeas and garden roses are usually the first to go. Wind is the other problem for outdoor ceremonies on open lawns and rooftops in Bricktown and Midtown, because it strips petals off fresh blooms. Faux stems hold their shape in both conditions, and that's the main reason couples rent instead of ordering fresh.

Bridal Bouquet Rental FAQ

Can I see the actual bouquet before my wedding day?

Yes — your consultation includes seeing real finished pieces, not just photos, so you know exactly what you're getting.

How do bridesmaid bouquets get returned after the wedding?

We arrange pickup after your event, or you can drop everything off at a scheduled time — whichever is easier for your wedding party.

What if I want to keep my bridal bouquet?

A purchase option is available — just let us know when you book and we'll build that into your quote.

How much does it cost to rent a wedding bouquet in Oklahoma City?

A rented bridal bouquet typically runs $85 to $225 depending on size and stem density, and bridesmaid bouquets run about $35 to $85 each. Those are estimates; the final price is confirmed in your written quote.

How far ahead should I book a bouquet rental?

For April through June or September through October dates, booking two to four months ahead gives time for a consultation and any special-order stems. Shorter timelines can work if the colors you want are on hand.

Will a rented bouquet hold up at an outdoor summer wedding?

Yes. Faux and real-touch stems don't wilt in heat or need water, so they look the same in late-afternoon portraits as they did at the first look. That is the main advantage over fresh flowers in Oklahoma summers.
